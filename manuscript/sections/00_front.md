## Abstract

**Background:** Handheld near-infrared (NIR) spectrometers are deployed as fleets of nominally identical units, yet one calibration, built on one unit, is used on all of them. Calibration transfer treats pairs of instruments, so fleet operators cannot know without laboratory analyses how accurate a calibration will be on unseen units, how to calibrate for the fleet, or whether a new unit is fit for use.

**Results:** Treating units as a random population, we derive a target-label-free estimator of the error of a linear calibration on a given unit and on unseen units, using only the master's cross-validation and replicate scans and items scanned on several units, and fleet-risk PLS (FR-PLS), whose regularisation weight follows from the risk it minimises. Across 30 handheld mango dry-matter units (leave-one-unit-out), fleet-level error predictions were within 1 % of realised errors and unit-level predictions agreed closely within a scanning session (r = 0.98). FR-PLS reduced the error on unseen units by a median of 36 % per unit, outperformed EPO and GLSW tuned by the same criterion, and the theoretical weight lay at the minimum of the error–weight curve. Five paired fruit scanned on a new unit supported a correct accept / correct / reject decision for 90 % of deployments. A soil fleet served as negative control; a corn benchmark confirmed the ranking.

**Significance:** Without laboratory analyses beyond the master's calibration set, calibrations can be released with their expected error on future units, built to minimise it, and each unit qualified from five paired scans.
