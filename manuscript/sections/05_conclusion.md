## 5. Conclusions

Handheld NIR spectrometers are deployed as fleets, yet calibration transfer has been studied as a problem between pairs of instruments. Treating the units of a fleet as a random sample from a population turns three questions that previously required reference analyses on every unit into quantities that can be computed from spectra. (i) A target-label-free estimator, built on a textbook error identity, predicts the RMSEP of a linear calibration on a specific unit and on units not yet seen. The fleet-level prediction for unseen mango units matched the realised error within 1 %; unit-level predictions agreed closely (r = 0.98) when the paired items were scanned in the same session as the routine samples, and remained unbiased but imprecise across sessions, because unit effects change between sessions. (ii) Minimising the fleet risk gives fleet-risk PLS, whose regularisation weight follows from that risk; on paired test fruit the weight lay at the minimum of the empirical error–weight curve. FR-PLS reduced the error on unseen units by a median of 36 % per unit, outperformed EPO and GLSW tuned by the same criterion, and did not differ significantly from a calibration requiring reference values for all fleet scans; its own fleet-risk prediction is optimistic by about 12 % (median ratio 0.88). (iii) Five items scanned on a new unit and on the master predict the unit's error (r = 0.95 within the session), remove most of it by bias correction, and supported an accept / correct / reject decision whose accept step was correct for 90 % of master-PLS deployments and reduced out-of-specification deployments from 66 % to 13 %. Paired spectra from even one other unit improve the calibration, but a claim of fleet accuracy should rest on at least four units. A MEMS FT-NIR soil fleet with small, unstructured unit effects behaved as a negative control.

The framework is linear, adds no hyper-parameter to the PLS workflow and uses data that fleet operators can collect at negligible cost. Its limitations follow from its assumptions: the fleet must be representative of future units (an atypical unit is detected by the qualification step, not prevented by FR-PLS); unit-specific risk predictions and bias corrections are session-specific and should be renewed when operating conditions change; and drift of a unit over time or across seasons, as well as changes of the sample population, are outside the present scope. Extending the population view to time — units as random draws in space, seasons as random draws in time — and to non-linear calibrations through the same identity applied to local linearisations are natural next steps.
