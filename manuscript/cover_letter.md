Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript **"Fleet calibration of handheld near-infrared spectrometers: label-free prediction and minimisation of the error on instrument units never seen in calibration"** for publication in *Analytica Chimica Acta*.

Handheld NIR spectrometers are now deployed as fleets of nominally identical units, but calibration transfer — including several recent contributions in *Analytica Chimica Acta* — is formulated for pairs of instruments and judged with reference values from the secondary instrument. Our manuscript introduces a different principle: the units of a fleet are treated as a random sample from a population. This yields:

1. a target-label-free estimator of the error a linear calibration will have on a specific unit and on units that have never been seen, computed from the master's cross-validation and replicate scans and from items scanned on several units;
2. fleet-risk PLS, whose regularisation weight follows from the risk it minimises rather than being tuned — and which lay at the minimum of the empirical error–weight curve;
3. a five-sample qualification rule (accept / bias-correct / reject) for a newly delivered unit, evaluated as a decision.

The framework is validated in leave-one-unit-out deployment on two public multi-unit data sets — 30 handheld mango dry-matter units over six seasons and a seven-unit handheld soil fleet that serves as a negative control — and on the classical corn benchmark, against EPO, GLSW, Tikhonov transfer, PDS, pooled multi-instrument calibration and oracle-tuned variants, with statistics computed per physical instrument and robustness checks for acquisition sessions, fruit populations and the choice of master. Fleet-level error predictions agreed with realised errors within 1 %, FR-PLS reduced the error on unseen units by a median of 36 % per unit, and five paired items supported correct qualification decisions for 90 % of unit deployments.

We believe the work fits the journal's emphasis on new chemometric principles with demonstrated analytical relevance: it turns multi-unit deployment of NIR calibrations into a quantified procedure that needs no laboratory analyses beyond the master's calibration set.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Related earlier work by the authors on prediction intervals for new instruments (Talanta, doi:10.1016/j.talanta.2026.130941) addresses a different question (interval coverage with labelled data from several instruments) and is cited. This research received no specific funding, and the authors declare no competing interests. All data are public (DOIs given in the manuscript); code will be made available on request.
