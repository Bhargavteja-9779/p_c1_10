## Key author-guideline elements (verified from the anatomy of 2025–2026 ACA full texts in Europe PMC)
- Structured abstract with headings **Background / Results / Significance** (also "Significance and novelty").
- **Graphical abstract** present in every inspected research article.
- Keywords list; Highlights (Elsevier standard: 3–5 bullets, <= 85 characters each) — highlights rule not verifiable from the blocked official guide; included to be safe.
- Body: Introduction → Materials and methods (Experimental) → Results and discussion → Conclusion(s).
- Back matter: CRediT authorship statement, Declaration of competing interest, Declaration of generative AI use (Elsevier-wide policy), Acknowledgements/Funding, **Data availability**, Supplementary data, References.
- References: numbered in order of citation, Elsevier numeric style: "[1] A.B. Author, C. Author, Title, J. Abbrev. Vol (Year) pages."

## Key rejection risks (journal-specific)
1. Desk rejection if the contribution reads as "applying known ML to a new dataset" (ACA requires new principles).
2. Chemometrics reviewers expect classical baselines (PLS with proper LV selection, PDS/DS/SBC for transfer) and correct validation (no leakage between replicates/seasons) — ACA itself has published validation-strategy tutorials in 2025–2026.
3. Need to demonstrate analytical relevance (real spectroscopic data, practical recalibration cost), not only ML metrics.
4. Timeline risk: 9.7% of accepted ACA papers took > 100 days; acceptance time for an individual manuscript cannot be guaranteed.
