### 1B. Item (1): label-free estimate of RMSEP on an unseen unit

|---|---|---|
| Olivieri et al. 2006 IUPAC report, *Pure Appl Chem* 78:633, doi:10.1351/pac200678030633; Faber & Kowalski 1997, *Appl Spectrosc* 51:660; Fernandez-Ahumada et al. 2012, *ACA* 721:28 | Prediction-uncertainty theory. Spectral error propagates to the prediction through b (variance term proportional to b' Sigma_x b), and RMSEP can be corrected for reference error. | **Already established for random noise: an error in x maps to a prediction error of b'dx.** The identity e_k = e_0 + b'D is therefore not new as algebra. **(a) Explicit check: "b'*Delta-x from paired standards" or "prediction bias from standardisation samples".** No paper was found that (i) predicts the RMSEP a model will have on a new instrument from paired-standard spectral differences via b'D, (ii) treats units as a random sample to estimate risk on an unseen unit, or (iii) corrects that estimate for replicate noise. Related practice exists:
- slope/bias correction (Bouveresse 1996) and virtual-standard SBC (Cooper 2011) predict standards on master and slave and regress one on the other;
- Hayes 2016 and Hayes 2019 use average difference spectra to adjust bias;
- Grelet 2017 reports RMS master-slave prediction differences;
- IUPAC/Olivieri 2006 gives propagation of random spectral error through b.

In short, the identity is implicit everywhere, but the *estimator for an unseen unit* was not found.

### 1C. Item (2): augmented PLS with a theory-fixed weight, and LV choice by fleet risk

| Ref | Summary | Overlap / difference |
|---|---|---|
| Kalivas et al. 2009, *Appl Spectrosc* 63:800, doi:10.1366/000370209788701206; Kunz et al. 2010 *Anal Chem* 82:3642 and *J Chemom* 24:218; Kalivas 2012 *J Chemom* 26:218 | Tikhonov-regularised transfer. Standardisation samples are **augmented** onto the calibration set with a defined weighting scheme. The TR and PLS forms are compared. The 2010 J Chemom paper studies standardisation-sample design. | **Mechanistically pre-empts augmentation.** Stacking difference spectra (target 0) into the least-squares problem is Kalivas-style TR transfer. Differences: their weights and lambda are *tuned* (harmonious/parsimonious trade-off or model-selection grids); they use one secondary instrument; and there is no population expectation. Novelty therefore rests on deriving the weight from E_k[(e_0 + b'D_k)^2] = sum e_0^2 + n b' Sigma_D b, a closed-form weight from the fleet-risk objective with no tuning. **Phrase it as "fleet-risk TR with a derived weight", not "a new augmented PLS".** |
| Ren et al. 2026, *Chemom Intell Lab Syst* 279:105901, doi:10.1016/j.chemolab.2026.105901 | "Instrument-shift-informed source-domain augmentation for calibration transfer in NIR." **No abstract from any API.** | Very likely augments the source data with estimated instrument shifts. It is a direct competitor for item (2) and is recent. **Get the full text** (library access) to check whether it uses multiple units, how it weights the augmentation and whether it estimates risk. |
| Andrew & Fearn 2004, *Chemom Intell Lab Syst* 72:51 (TOP); Roger et al. 2003 (EPO); Martens et al. 2003 (GLSW basis); Wold 1998 / Sjöblom 1998 (OSC) | Project out, or down-weight, the subspace of between-instrument (or nuisance) difference spectra. TOP explicitly builds it from several instruments. | EPO and TOP are the hard-projection limit of our penalty, and GLSW is a whitening variant. All need a chosen rank or alpha. Ours is a soft penalty with a weight set by the risk, and EPO/TOP/GLSW are baselines. |
| Nikzad-Langerodi & Sobieczky 2021, *J Chemom* 35:e3319 (graph-based CT); Nikzad-Langerodi et al. 2018, *Anal Chem* 90:6693 (di-PLS); Mikulasek et al. 2023, *J Chemom* 37:e3477 (mdi-PLS) | Graph-based CT adds a PLS penalty that makes the latent projections of *paired standards measured on two instruments* invariant. di-PLS and mdi-PLS align domain moments across any number of domains. | Tillmann & Paul 1998, *JNIRS* (WinISI "repeatability file"; DOI 10.1255/jnirs.122 found, not added to the JSON) | Adds within-sample spectral differences under a nuisance factor (moisture) to the calibration with zero target. | An old practical form of the same idea: difference rows with target zero. | Heinze-Deml & Meinshausen 2021, *Mach Learn* 110:303 (CoRe); Rothenhäusler et al. 2021, *JRSS-B* 83:215 (anchor regression) | CoRe penalises the conditional variance of predictions across multiple observations of the *same object* (an ID variable), which is exactly the paired-item structure. ### 1D. Item (3): label-free qualification of a new unit from a few paired items

- **ASTM D6122-18** (doi:10.1520/d6122-18) and **ASTM E1655-17** (doi:10.1520/e1655-17): standard practices to validate and qualify multivariate IR analysers using validation samples. These need reference values or a reference analyser, and their acceptance is based on bias and precision tests.
- **Mark & Workman, Grelet 2017, Bland-Altman 1986, Schuirmann 1987 (TOST)**: paired-agreement and equivalence frameworks.
- **Spiers 2021**: label-free model updating and selection on target samples, but no accept/reject rule.

No paper was found that gives a *label-free accept / bias-correct / recalibrate rule for a new unit, based on predicted risk with a sample-size rule for m*. This item is novel in formulation but builds on standard pieces (paired differences, equivalence testing). Present it as a decision rule with known error rates (simulated and empirical), not as a new statistical test.

### 1E. Item (4): learning curves for number of fleet units N and paired items m

- **Kunz et al. 2010 J Chemom**: standardisation-sample design.
- **Andries & Vander Heyden 2024 ABC** and the OPCS papers: choice of transfer subsets.
- General guidance that PDS needs few transfer samples (Zhang 2022; Ciza 2022).

No paper was found on *how many instrument units* must be in the training fleet for generalisation to unseen units in NIR. Domain-generalisation surveys (Zhou et al. 2022, *IEEE TPAMI*) discuss the number of source domains only qualitatively. This item appears **novel**.

## 2. Verdict

| Item | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Unbiased label-free estimate of RMSEP on an unseen unit (fleet risk), replicate-noise corrected | **Novel (with partial conceptual precedent)** | The identity e = e_0 + b'dx is textbook (IUPAC 2006; NAS). Paired master/slave prediction differences are routine (SBC, Grelet 2017, Mark & Workman). No work was found that treats units as a random population and gives an unbiased estimator of the error on an *unseen* unit, with replicate correction and unit-bootstrap CIs. The early result that unit-specific estimates track realised RMSE across 31 mango units is the key evidence. |
| (2) Augmented PLS with a theory-fixed weight; LV selection by fleet risk | **Partially pre-empted** | Augmenting with difference/standardisation rows is Kalivas TR (2009-2012), with EPO/TOP/GLSW as projection limits, graph-CT and CoRe as paired-invariance penalties, and Ren 2026 (augmentation, unread). What remains novel: (i) the weight *derived* from fleet risk (no tuning), (ii) multi-unit Sigma_D estimated with shrinkage, and (iii) choosing LVs by unbiased predicted fleet risk rather than heuristics (Spiers MDPS) or variable robustness (Swierenga 2000). |
| (3) Label-free unit qualification test from a few paired items | **Novel in formulation, built from known parts** | ASTM D6122/E1655 qualification needs reference values. Paired agreement and TOST are standard. A risk-based accept / bias-correct / recalibrate rule with a sample-size rule for m was not found. |
| (4) Learning curves for N units and m paired items | **Novel** | Only transfer-sample design (Kunz 2010; Andries 2024) exists. Nothing was found on the number of fleet units needed. |

## 3. Risks and suggested sharper angles

1. **Do not claim the identity or augmentation as new.** Claim instead: "the deployment error on a *random unseen unit* is identifiable without labels, and here is an unbiased, noise-corrected estimator with a CI". Put Kalivas 2009, Andrew & Fearn 2004, Nikzad-Langerodi 2021 and Heinze-Deml 2021 next to each other and show that the fleet-risk derivation *fixes* the weight they tune. Include an experiment: tuned TR (oracle lambda) vs the derived weight. If the derived weight matches the oracle, that is the headline.
2. **Ren 2026 CILS is the main unread threat** for item (2). Get it before writing the Introduction.
3. **Grelet 2017 (66 MIR milk instruments) is the largest fleet precedent.** Position fleet risk as complementary to per-unit standardisation: it predicts *before standardising* which units need standardising.
4. **Heterogeneous vs homogeneous fleets.** The near-homogeneous NeoSpectra fleet sits in tension with Mitu 2024, which reports significant cross-unit loss on 5 NeoSpectra units. Explain it in terms of property, pre-processing and replicate noise. Present the soil fleet as a *negative control*: the estimator should correctly predict that little is gained. This becomes a strength.
5. **Sharper novelty angle:** the "risk audit before deployment" workflow. Estimate fleet risk, then qualify each unit, then decide where to spend labels. Pair it with the unit-count learning curves as design laws for vendors and users (Walsh/F750, Si-Ware). Compared with the Talanta 2026 group-conformal paper, the step forward is moving from labelled intervals to label-free risk.
6. The Si-Ware chapter (Hossam 2020) and the agriRxiv LMM preprint (Potash 2026) should be read in full. OpenAlex queries cut short by rate limits should be re-run once before submission.
